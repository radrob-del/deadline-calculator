FRCP 6(a) Deadline Calculator

Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, Rule 6(a): computing time periods stated in days.

Educational demo. Not legal advice. Verify every deadline against the current rule, the governing statute or order, local rules, and the court's calendar before relying on it. The text of Rule 6(a)(5) and 6(a)(6) quoted below was not verified against a live source when this tool was built.

Rule language this tool relies on

6(a)(1) (confirmed against published text)

"(A) exclude the day of the event that triggers the period; (B) count every day, including intermediate Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays; and (C) include the last day of the period, but if the last day is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the period continues to run until the end of the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday."

6(a)(5) (from memory; unverified)

"The 'next day' is determined by continuing to count forward when the period is measured after an event and backward when measured before an event."

6(a)(6) (from memory; unverified)

"'Legal holiday' means: (A) the day set aside by statute for observing New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr.'s Birthday, Washington's Birthday, Memorial Day, Juneteenth National Independence Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Columbus Day, Veterans' Day, Thanksgiving Day, or Christmas Day; (B) any day declared a holiday by the President or Congress; and (C) for periods that are measured after an event, any other day declared a holiday by the state where the district court is located."

Not handled: periods stated in hours (6(a)(2)), the last-day cutoff time (6(a)(4)), an inaccessible clerk's office (6(a)(3)), and the 3-day mail extension in Rule 6(d) (which, as of 2016, no longer applies to electronic service and has changed over time). Check those by hand.
